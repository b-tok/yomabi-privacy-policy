Yomabi Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 24, 2026

This Privacy Policy describes how Yomabi ("the App", "we", "us") collects, uses, and protects information when you use our mobile applications for Android and iOS. Yomabi is a Japanese dictionary and learning app that provides dictionary lookups, OCR-based text recognition, flashcard-based spaced repetition, and other language learning tools.

In short: Yomabi is designed with privacy in mind. All your personal study data stays on your device. Analytics are disabled by default and require your explicit opt-in. No user accounts or server-side storage exist.

1. Information We Collect

1.1 Data Stored Locally on Your Device

The following data is stored entirely on your device and is never transmitted to any server:

  • Search history
  • Favorites and bookmarks
  • Flashcards, decks, and SRS (spaced repetition) progress
  • Flashcard image and audio attachments, including pictures of your screen that you add to flashcards from Popup Dictionary (Section 1.10)
  • User preferences and settings
  • In-app purchase status

This data is stored in on-device databases and local preference storage (Room / DataStore on Android, equivalent local storage on iOS). You have full control over this data and can delete it at any time from within the app, by clearing the app's data in your device settings (Android), or by uninstalling the app.

1.2 Firebase Analytics (Opt-In)

Yomabi integrates Firebase Analytics to help us understand how the app is used and to improve the experience. Analytics collection is disabled by default. On first launch, a consent dialog asks whether you would like to enable anonymous usage analytics. You can change this choice at any time in the app's Settings.

When enabled, Firebase Analytics collects:

  • Anonymous usage events (e.g., screen views, searches, flashcard creation, OCR usage)
  • General device information (device model, OS version, screen size)
  • App version and session duration

This data is anonymous and aggregated. It does not include your search queries, flashcard content, or any personal study data.

1.3 Firebase Crashlytics (Legitimate Interest)

Yomabi uses Firebase Crashlytics to collect crash reports and stability data so we can identify and fix bugs. Crashlytics runs under our legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) in maintaining the app's stability and is not gated by the usage analytics toggle.

Crashlytics collects:

  • Crash stack traces and error logs
  • Device model and manufacturer
  • Operating system version
  • App version
  • A Firebase Installation ID (see Section 1.5)

Crash data does not include any of your personal study content, search queries, or flashcard data. To object to this processing under Art. 21 GDPR, please contact us using the address in Section 10; you can also clear the app's data to reset the FID or uninstall the app to stop all future crash reports.

1.4 Firebase Remote Config (Legitimate Interest)

Yomabi uses Firebase Remote Config to adjust certain non-personal app parameters without requiring a new app release — for example, the daily free OCR lookup limit, the number of rewarded ads available per day, whether the Plus upgrade prompt is shown, and the minimum supported app version.

Remote Config fetches run at app start on the legal basis of our legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)) in being able to operate and tune the app's configuration without shipping a new release for every change. Each fetch transmits a Firebase Installation ID (see Section 1.5) and basic app / SDK metadata (package name, app version, locale) to Google. No behavioural analytics, no search queries, and no personal study content are involved. To object under Art. 21 GDPR, please contact us using the address in Section 10; you can also clear the app's data to reset the FID or uninstall the app to stop all future requests.

1.5 Firebase Installation ID

Any Firebase service that makes a network request — Crashlytics and Remote Config (always on under legitimate interest) and Analytics (only after you opt in) — transmits a Firebase Installation ID (FID). The FID is a randomly generated identifier that represents your installation of Yomabi. Google uses it to associate requests from the same installation across the Firebase services that are active.

The FID is reset if you clear the app's data or reinstall Yomabi. It is not linked to your Google account and is not shared with any third party other than Google.

1.6 Google AdMob and User Messaging Platform (UMP)

Yomabi displays rewarded video ads (via Google AdMob) that provide bonus OCR lookups. The AdMob SDK may collect certain data in accordance with Google's advertising policies, including:

  • Device identifiers (e.g., Advertising ID on Android, IDFA on iOS)
  • Ad interaction data (views, clicks)
  • General device and network information
  • Coarse location derived from IP address

Consent (EEA / UK / Switzerland). In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland, Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) shows a consent dialog on first launch that lets you choose whether Google and its ad partners may process your data for personalized advertising and related purposes. If you decline, non-personalized ads are shown instead. You can re-open this consent form at any time via Settings → Ad Privacy Settings.

App Tracking Transparency (iOS). On iOS 14.5 and later, before any ad-tracking identifier (IDFA) is read, the operating system shows a system-level “Allow Tracking?” prompt (App Tracking Transparency). If you choose “Ask App Not to Track”, the IDFA is not shared and non-personalized ads are shown.

US state privacy laws (CCPA / CPRA and similar). Residents of California and other US states with comparable privacy laws may use the same UMP consent form to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information for targeted advertising. Selecting the “Do not sell or share my personal information” option in that form instructs Google and its ad partners to apply the Global Privacy Control / US opt-out signal. Yomabi itself does not sell personal information.

Data collected by AdMob is processed by Google. Please refer to Google's Privacy Policy for details on how Google handles this information.

1.7 In-App Purchases (Google Play Billing / Apple In-App Purchase)

Yomabi offers in-app purchases (Pro upgrade and optional tips) through the platform's billing system — Google Play Billing on Android and Apple In-App Purchase (StoreKit) on iOS. Purchase transactions are handled by Google or Apple respectively. Purchase tokens or transaction identifiers are stored locally on your device only. We do not operate any server that processes or stores payment information.

1.8 Bug Reports (User-Initiated)

If you tap Send Feedback in Settings and choose to submit a bug report, the following is transmitted to a Google Apps Script endpoint we control:

  • The text description you typed
  • A random per-install identifier so we can group related reports
  • Device model, OS version, app version, and locale
  • A timestamp

Bug reports are only sent when you explicitly tap the submit button. The payload is signed with HMAC-SHA256 before transmission so we can reject forged submissions. The random per-install identifier resets when you clear app data or reinstall Yomabi.

1.9 Voice Search (Optional)

Voice search is off by default. When you turn on Voice Input in Settings and tap the microphone button, the audio from your microphone is captured while voice search is listening and passed to a speech recognition service, which turns it into text. Yomabi uses the recognized text as your search. Normally the speech recognition service records the audio itself. On Android 13 and later, if Android silences the speech recognition service's own recording, Yomabi records the audio itself and passes it straight to the speech recognition service while you speak. Either way, Yomabi does not store the audio and does not send it to its own servers.

Android, online. When your device is connected to the internet, voice search normally uses Google's speech recognition service on your device (for example, Speech Recognition & Synthesis from Google), which sends the audio to Google's servers to turn it into text. This is the same when Yomabi records the audio and passes it on. Google processes the audio under Google's Privacy Policy and may do so on servers outside your country, including in the United States (see Section 3). The first time voice search sends your voice to Google from within Yomabi, Yomabi shows a short notice that says so. If your device uses a speech recognition service from another company, that company's privacy policy applies instead.

Android, Google's voice input window. On some phones, Android doesn't let Google's speech recognition service use the microphone while Yomabi is on screen. Voice search then opens Google's own voice input window instead. That window is part of Google's app: it records your voice itself, under Google's Privacy Policy, and gives Yomabi only the recognized text. When your device is offline, or when Offline only is on, Yomabi asks the window to recognize your speech on your device.

Android, offline. When your device is offline, or when you turn on Offline only under Voice Input in Settings, voice search uses the Japanese voice pack on your device. The audio stays on your device and is not sent to Google or anyone else.

iOS. On iOS, voice search uses Apple's speech recognition (SFSpeechRecognizer). Yomabi does not require on-device recognition, so iOS may send the audio to Apple's servers to turn it into text, under Apple's Privacy Policy. Yomabi uses the recognized text as your search and does not store the audio.

Microphone indicator (Android). In two cases the microphone indicator may light up briefly while no voice is being recognized:

  • If you tap the microphone button while the microphone is blocked with Android's privacy toggle, Yomabi briefly opens the microphone so that Android shows its “Unblock device microphone?” prompt. Yomabi reads nothing from it, and while the microphone is blocked Android provides only silence.
  • On some phones, shortly after you download the Japanese voice pack, Yomabi checks that the pack is installed by running the speech recognition service on one second of generated silence. The microphone is not used for this check.

1.10 Popup Dictionary (Optional, Android)

Popup Dictionary lets you look up Japanese words shown in other apps, for example in a video or a manga reader. It is off by default. You turn it on in Yomabi's Settings and start it from its Quick Settings tile, from a button on Yomabi's home screen, or from the Yomabi app icon's shortcut. You can stop it at any time with the Stop button in its notification, with the tile, or in Yomabi.

  • Screen capture. Each time you start Popup Dictionary, Android asks whether Yomabi may capture your screen (on newer Android versions, the entire screen or a single app), and Android shows that the screen is being shared while it runs. Yomabi keeps only the newest picture of the screen in memory and replaces it as the screen changes. When you tap the Yomabi button, Yomabi reads the Japanese text in that picture on your device (on-device OCR) and looks the words up in its on-device dictionary. So that its button can step aside while a video plays, Yomabi also compares small, low-resolution copies of the screen in memory and checks whether any app is playing media (Android doesn't tell it which app).
  • What stays where. Screen pictures and the text read from them are processed only on your device and kept in memory only while they are needed. They are never sent to us or to anyone else, and they are not saved to your photo gallery. Two things you start yourself go further: when you add a word to a flashcard deck with Plus, the card gets the picture of the screen as its picture (you can crop or remove it), stored in Yomabi's storage on your device like other flashcard images; and when you open a word or a selected text in Yomabi's search, it becomes a normal search (Sections 1.1 and 1.2).
  • Display over other apps. Popup Dictionary shows its floating Yomabi button and the lookup card on top of the app you are using. Android asks you once to allow this in its settings. Yomabi uses it only to draw its own button and card.
  • Notification. While Popup Dictionary runs, Android requires a notification (“Popup Dictionary is running”), which also offers Scan and Stop buttons.
  • Lookups and ads. For free users, Popup Dictionary lookups count toward the same daily allowance as camera scans, and the rewarded ads that add lookups are the same Google AdMob ads described in Section 1.6. For free users, turning Popup Dictionary on first shows Google's ad consent form where Google requires one.

4. Data Retention and Deletion

All user study data (search history, favorites, flashcards, SRS progress) is stored locally on your device and is retained until you choose to delete it. You can delete your data at any time by:

  • Using in-app features to clear individual items (e.g., deleting flashcard decks, clearing search history)
  • Clearing the app's data in your device settings (Android)
  • Uninstalling the app (on both Android and iOS, uninstalling removes all locally stored data)

For data collected by Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, Google's standard data retention policies apply. Firebase Analytics data is retained for up to 14 months by default. Crashlytics data is retained for 90 days.

Bug report submissions are retained in a private Google Sheet for as long as we consider them useful for diagnosing the reported issue, and may be deleted on request using the address in Section 10.

5. Your Rights Under the GDPR (European Users)

If you are located in the European Economic Area (EEA), the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, you have the following rights under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) / UK GDPR:

  • Right of access (Art. 15): You may ask what personal data we process about you. Because personal study data lives only on your device, you already have full access via the app. For anything we hold server-side (bug reports you submitted), contact us using Section 10.
  • Right to rectification (Art. 16): You may correct inaccurate data. Study data is editable in-app; for bug-report contents, contact us.
  • Right to erasure / “right to be forgotten” (Art. 17): You may clear app data or uninstall Yomabi to erase everything stored on-device. For server-held bug reports, contact us.
  • Right to restriction of processing (Art. 18): You may ask us to limit how we process your data. Contact us using Section 10.
  • Right to data portability (Art. 20): Because study data stays on-device, you can already export flashcards and decks in-app. For anything else, contact us.
  • Right to object (Art. 21): You may object to processing based on legitimate interest — specifically Crashlytics and Remote Config. Contact us and we will stop processing; you can also clear app data (which resets the FID) or uninstall (which stops all future requests).
  • Right to withdraw consent: For Analytics (consent-based), you may withdraw consent at any time in Settings.
  • Right to lodge a complaint (Art. 77): You may complain to the supervisory authority in your member state.

Data controller for GDPR purposes: Boris Tokic. Contact details in Section 10.

Legal bases (summary):

  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a)): Firebase Analytics, personalized advertising (where UMP / ATT consent is given)
  • Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)): Firebase Crashlytics, Firebase Remote Config, non-personalized advertising, processing of user-submitted bug reports
  • Performance of a contract (Art. 6(1)(b)): processing of in-app purchases by Google Play / Apple; turning your speech into text when you start a voice search (Section 1.9)

6. Your Rights Under US State Privacy Laws (CCPA / CPRA / etc.)

If you are a resident of California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah, or another US state with a comparable consumer privacy law, you have the following rights:

  • Right to know what personal information is collected about you
  • Right to delete personal information we hold
  • Right to correct inaccurate information
  • Right to opt out of sale or sharing of personal information for targeted advertising
  • Right to non-discrimination for exercising these rights

How to exercise opt-out. In the AdMob consent form (Settings → Ad Privacy Settings) select the “Do not sell or share my personal information” option. We also honor the Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal where it is available.

Do Not Sell My Personal Information. Yomabi itself does not sell personal information. AdMob may share device identifiers with ad partners for personalized advertising; opting out via the UMP form stops this sharing.

To exercise any of the above rights, contact us using the address in Section 10.

7. Children's Privacy

Yomabi is not directed at children under the age of 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has provided data through the app, please contact us and we will take steps to address the situation.

8. Security

Because Yomabi stores all user data locally on your device, the security of your data depends on your device's own security measures (screen lock, encryption, etc.). We recommend keeping your device's operating system up to date and using a secure lock screen.

Data transmitted to Firebase, AdMob, and our bug-report endpoint is encrypted in transit using industry-standard protocols (TLS/SSL). Bug-report submissions are additionally signed with HMAC-SHA256.
